## T1. Freeman 2008 — Pregabalin pooled 7-RCT dose-ranging analysis (register ID C1)

- **Citation (confirmed, not garbled):** Freeman R, Durso-Decruz E, Emir B. "Efficacy, safety, and tolerability of pregabalin treatment for painful diabetic peripheral neuropathy: findings from seven randomized, controlled trials across a range of doses." *Diabetes Care.* 2008;31(7):1448–1454.
- **DOI:** 10.2337/dc07-2105 — PMID: 18356405
- **Status:** `ACCESS_VERIFIED` (PubMed abstract record opened directly: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18356405/)
- **Correction to register:** author list is **not** garbled — "Freeman R; Durso-Decruz E; Emir B" confirmed exactly as cited in `02_SOURCE-INVENTORY.md`/register.
- **Design:** Pooled (post hoc) analysis of 7 double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trials of pregabalin 150/300/600 mg/day (TID or BID) for painful DPN. Only 1 trial used all 3 doses; TID dosing used in 4 trials. Treatment duration 5–13 weeks.
- **Population:** Painful diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN) — pregabalin-specific, DPN-specific (not mixed neuropathic pain etiologies).
- **Key results (verbatim from abstract):**
  - Pregabalin significantly reduced pain and pain-related sleep interference vs placebo at 150, 300, 600 mg/day TID (all P ≤ 0.007).
  - Only 600 mg/day showed efficacy when given BID (P ≤ 0.001).
  - Effect appears dose-related; greatest effect at 600 mg/day.
  - Kaplan-Meier median time to onset of sustained (≥30% at endpoint) 1-point improvement: 4 days (600 mg/day), 5 days (300 mg/day), 13 days (150 mg/day), 60 days (placebo).
  - Most common treatment-emergent AEs: dizziness, somnolence, peripheral edema.
- **Evidence classification:** `DIRECT EVIDENCE` (pregabalin-specific, DPN-specific, placebo-controlled, pooled RCT data) for pain/sleep efficacy and dose–response/time-to-effect. Note: this is an industry-relevant pooled/post hoc analysis of predominantly older, short-duration (5–13 week) trials — funding/COI and individual-trial risk of bias not stated in abstract; `NEEDS_FULLTEXT` for funding disclosure, individual trial N, and risk-of-bias assessment before final synthesis language.
- **Sponsor-bias note:** This is the single most favorable pregabalin-specific efficacy source identified so far; must be balanced against T3 (class-level NNT/NNH from an independent, non-industry NeuPSIG synthesis) and against comparative/negative findings in T5–T6 before any synthesis claim of superiority.

## T2. Frontiers in Endocrinology 2026 — Pregabalin + duloxetine combination SR/MA (register ID C4)

- **Citation:** Shi Y, Chen Y, Zhao H. "Pregabalin and duloxetine combination for painful diabetic neuropathy: a systematic review and meta-analysis." *Frontiers in Endocrinology.* 2026;17:1750441.
- **DOI:** 10.3389/fendo.2026.1750441 — Registration: PROSPERO CRD420251179997 (confirmed directly in the primary abstract, resolving the register's "unconfirmed" flag)
- **Status:** `ACCESS_VERIFIED` (Crossref DOI record opened directly, full structured abstract retrieved — this is the article's own abstract, not a search-snippet paraphrase)
- **Design:** Systematic search of multiple databases (inception to 2025-11-01) for RCTs of pregabalin + duloxetine vs monotherapy in painful diabetic neuropathy. Cochrane RoB 2.0 used; RevMan 5.4. **3 RCTs, 471 patients total included.**
- **Key results (verbatim numeric tokens from primary abstract):**
  - Pooled (2 poolable studies): combination > monotherapy pain relief, **MD = −1.82, 95% CI −2.10 to −1.54, P < 0.00001**.
  - VAS (single study): **MD = −1.42, 95% CI −1.83 to −1.01, P < 0.00001**.
  - BPI-MSF (single study): **MD = −1.46, 95% CI −2.35 to −0.57, P = 0.001**.
  - PDQ (single study): **MD = −3.00, 95% CI −5.55 to −0.45, P = 0.02**.
  - ≥50% pain reduction, combination vs duloxetine 120 mg alone: **RR = 1.81, 95% CI 1.17–2.81, P = 0.008**.
  - Overall AE risk: **RR = 1.10, 95% CI 0.84–1.46, P = 0.48** (not significant).
  - Somnolence: **RR = 0.79, 95% CI 0.30–2.08, P = 0.63**. Nausea/vomiting: **RR = 2.02, 95% CI 0.77–5.27, P = 0.15**.
  - GRADE certainty: **very low to low for most outcomes.**
  - Conclusion (verbatim): "Low-certainty evidence suggests that pregabalin plus duloxetine may improve short-term pain scores compared with monotherapy in painful diabetic neuropathy. Safety outcomes remain uncertain due to few trials and imprecision."
- **Included-trial example noted in register (not yet independently re-verified at its own primary record this wave):** QI 2022, n=51 vs 51, pregabalin 75 mg BID + duloxetine 30 mg BID vs duloxetine 30 mg BID alone, 4-week treatment. `NEEDS_FULLTEXT`/`NEEDS_SOURCE` to independently open the QI 2022 primary trial before treating its numbers as separately citable.
- **Evidence classification:** `DIRECT EVIDENCE` for the specific combination-vs-monotherapy comparison, but **low certainty (GRADE)** and based on only 2–3 short-duration trials (471 patients) — must be reported with its certainty rating attached every time, per non-negotiable rule against omitting reasonable uncertainty. Register wording preserved exactly; not strengthened.
- **Sponsor-bias note:** This SR is the newest and most favorable combination-specific result. Flag strongly for moderator: certainty is GRADE **low**, N is small (471 across 3 trials, only 2 poolable for the primary pain endpoint), and it should never be presented as "proven synergy" — present as a hypothesis-generating signal only.

## T3. Soliman et al. 2025 (NeuPSIG update) — general neuropathic pain SR/MA (register ID C2)

- **Citation:** Soliman N, Moisset X, Ferraro MC, et al. (NeuPSIG). "Pharmacotherapy and non-invasive neuromodulation for neuropathic pain: a systematic review and meta-analysis." *Lancet Neurology.* 2025;24(5):413–428.
- **DOI:** 10.1016/S1474-4422(25)00068-7 — PMID: 40252663 — PROSPERO CRD42023389375
- **Status:** `ACCESS_VERIFIED` (PubMed abstract record opened directly: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40252663/; full text not opened this wave — `NEEDS_FULLTEXT` if DPN-specific subgroup data are required)
- **Design:** NeuPSIG update to its 2015 neuropathic-pain treatment recommendations. Systematic search (Embase, PubMed, ICTRP, ClinicalTrials.gov) — neuromodulation trials from inception, pharmacological trials from 2013-01-01 to 2024-02-12. Included double-blind RCTs ≥3 weeks treatment/follow-up, ≥10 participants/group. **313 trials (284 pharmacological, 29 neuromodulation), 48,789 participants.**
- **Critical population/scope finding — this is NOT DPN-specific:** trials included participants with neuropathic pain of **any** cause per IASP definition (mixed etiologies pooled across the whole review), explicitly **excluding** enriched-enrolment-randomised-withdrawal designs and non-neuropathic conditions (CRPS, non-radicular low back pain, fibromyalgia, idiopathic orofacial pain) — but not restricted to DPN. **Applying this review's numbers specifically to DPN is class-level/indirect extrapolation, not a DPN-specific direct estimate**, unless/until a DPN-only subgroup analysis is located in the full text.
- **Critical drug-identity finding — pregabalin is NOT reported separately:** pregabalin is pooled inside the **"α2δ-ligands" class** (i.e., gabapentin + pregabalin combined). The abstract reports no pregabalin-only NNT/NNH. **Any synthesis statement must say "α2δ-ligands (gabapentinoids, pooling gabapentin and pregabalin)," never "pregabalin," when citing this source's numbers.**
- **Key results (verbatim numeric tokens):**
  - TCAs: **NNT = 4.6 (95% CI 3.2–7.7), NNH = 17.1 (11.4–33.6), moderate certainty.**
  - **α2δ-ligands: NNT = 8.9 (95% CI 7.4–11.10), NNH = 26.2 (20.4–36.5), moderate certainty.**
  - SNRIs: **NNT = 7.4 (95% CI 5.6–10.9), NNH = 13.9 (10.9–19.0), moderate certainty.**
  - BTX-A: NNT = 2.7 (1.8–9.61), NNH = 216.3 (23.5–∞), moderate certainty.
  - Capsaicin 8% patch: NNT = 13.2 (7.6–50.8), NNH = 1129.3 (135.7–∞), moderate certainty.
  - Opioids: NNT = 5.9 (4.1–10.7), NNH = 15.4 (10.8–24.0), **low certainty.**
  - rTMS: NNT = 4.2 (2.3–28.3), NNH = 651.6 (34.7–∞), low certainty.
  - Capsaicin cream: NNT = 6.1 (3.1–∞), NNH = 18.6 (10.6–77.1), very low certainty.
  - Lidocaine 5% plasters: NNT = 14.5 (7.8–108.2), NNH = 178.0 (23.9–∞), very low certainty.
  - Recommendations: **strong** for TCAs, α2δ-ligands, SNRIs as first-line; **weak** for capsaicin 8% patch, capsaicin cream, lidocaine 5% plasters as second-line; **weak** for BTX-A, rTMS, opioids as third-line.
  - Conclusion (verbatim, partial): "Treatment outcomes are modest and for some treatments uncertainty remains."
- **Evidence classification:** `GUIDELINE / CONSENSUS` (this is the primary evidence base underlying a NeuPSIG guideline update) at the neuropathic-pain-class level; `INDIRECT EVIDENCE` specifically for DPN and specifically for pregabalin (vs gabapentin), because the reported class NNT/NNH pools both drug and pools all neuropathic-pain etiologies together.
- **Sponsor-bias note — important:** α2δ-ligand NNT (8.9) is numerically **worse** (higher = less efficient) than TCA NNT (4.6) and worse than SNRI NNT (7.4) in this synthesis, though all three receive the same "strong, first-line" recommendation grade. This is a directly relevant counter-balance to any moderator framing that implies pregabalin/gabapentinoids are more effective than TCAs or SNRIs — the guideline strength is equivalent across all three classes even though the point-estimate NNT ranks α2δ-ligands last of the three first-line classes. Must be presented, not omitted, per sponsor-bias guardrail.

## T5. COMBO-DN — head-to-head + combination RCT (named lead from MEDNOTE-002, now located at primary record)

- **Citation:** Tesfaye S, Wilhelm S, Lledó A, Schacht A, Tölle T, Bouhassira D, Cruccu G, Skljarevski V, Freynhagen R. "Duloxetine and pregabalin: high-dose monotherapy or their combination? The 'COMBO-DN study'—a multinational, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study in patients with diabetic peripheral neuropathic pain." *Pain.* 2013;154(12):2616–2625.
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.pain.2013.05.043 — PMID: 23732189
- **Status:** `ACCESS_VERIFIED` (PubMed abstract record opened directly: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23732189/)
- **Design:** Multicentre, double-blind, parallel-group RCT. Phase 1 (8 wk): duloxetine 60 mg/day vs pregabalin 300 mg/day. Phase 2 (8 wk, nonresponders only): duloxetine 120 mg/day (monotherapy high-dose) vs duloxetine 60 mg + pregabalin 300 mg (combination) vs pregabalin 600 mg/day (monotherapy high-dose). **N = 804 evaluated for initial therapy; 339 for combination/high-dose therapy.**
- **Key results (verbatim numeric tokens):**
  - Primary outcome (BPI-MSF 24h average pain change, combination [pooled] vs high-dose monotherapy [pooled]): mean change combination **−2.35** vs high-dose monotherapy **−2.16**; **P = 0.370** — **not statistically significant.**
  - 50% response rates: combination **52.1%** vs high-dose monotherapy **39.3%**; **P = 0.068** — not statistically significant.
  - Exploratory, uncorrected for multiple comparisons: duloxetine 60 mg/day superior to pregabalin 300 mg/day, **P < 0.001**.
  - Both drugs and combination "well tolerated."
- **Evidence classification:** `DIRECT EVIDENCE`, DPN-specific, head-to-head comparative (duloxetine vs pregabalin) and combination-vs-high-dose-monotherapy RCT.
- **Critical caution for synthesis (must not be silently softened):** the abstract's own framing is that combination was "not significantly superior to high-dose monotherapy" for the primary outcome, and the duloxetine>pregabalin exploratory result was explicitly uncorrected for multiplicity. Any moderator-facing claim that "combination therapy is proven better" or "duloxetine is proven superior to pregabalin" based on this trial alone would misstate the trial's own reported statistics — flag as `SOURCE_CONFLICT`-adjacent risk if any downstream draft overstates this.

## T6. OPTION-DM — three-pathway crossover RCT (named lead from MEDNOTE-002, now located at primary record)

- **Citation (main trial report):** Tesfaye S, Sloan G, Petrie J, White D, Bradburn M, Julious S, et al. "Comparison of amitriptyline supplemented with pregabalin, pregabalin supplemented with amitriptyline, and duloxetine supplemented with pregabalin for the treatment of diabetic peripheral neuropathic pain (OPTION-DM): a multicentre, double-blind, randomised crossover trial." *Lancet.* 2022;400(10353):680–690.
- **DOI:** 10.1016/S0140-6736(22)01472-6 — PMID: 36007534. Registry: ISRCTN17545443; EudraCT 2016-003146-89.
- **Companion HTA monograph (cost-effectiveness detail):** Tesfaye S, et al. "Optimal pharmacotherapy pathway in adults with diabetic peripheral neuropathic pain: the OPTION-DM RCT." *Health Technol Assess.* 2022;26(39). DOI: 10.3310/RXUO6757 — PMID: 36259684.
- **Protocol reference:** Selvarajah D, et al. *Trials.* 2018;19:566. DOI: 10.1186/s13063-018-2959-y — PMID: 30348206.
- **Status:** `ACCESS_VERIFIED` (all three PubMed abstract records opened directly)
- **Design:** Multicentre, randomised, double-blind, crossover trial, 13 UK centres (main paper) / 21 secondary-care centres (HTA report — `NEEDS_FULLTEXT` to reconcile 13 vs 21 site-count discrepancy between the two primary abstracts; not silently resolved here). Six ordered sequences of 3 pathways, each 16 weeks: amitriptyline supplemented with pregabalin (A–P), pregabalin supplemented with amitriptyline (P–A), duloxetine supplemented with pregabalin (D–P). Monotherapy 6 weeks; combination added at week 6 if NRS > 3. Doses titrated to max tolerated (amitriptyline 75 mg/day, duloxetine 120 mg/day, pregabalin 600 mg/day).
- **Population:** N = 140 randomised, 130 started a pathway, 84 completed ≥2 pathways (analysed for primary outcome). Eligibility: DPNP with mean daily pain NRS ≥ 4 (0–10 scale).
- **Key results (verbatim numeric tokens, main paper):**
  - NRS decreased from mean **6.6 (SD 1.5)** at baseline to **3.3 (1.8)** at week 16 in all three pathways (i.e., pathways converged, no ordering advantage reported at endpoint).
  - Pathway comparisons at week 16 (mean difference, 98.3% CI): D-P vs A-P **−0.1 (−0.5 to 0.3)**; P-A vs A-P **−0.1 (−0.5 to 0.3)**; P-A vs D-P **0.0 (−0.4 to 0.4)** — **none significant.**
  - Combination-phase NRS reduction **1.0 (SD 1.3)** vs remaining-on-monotherapy reduction **0.2 (1.5)**.
  - AEs were "predictable for the monotherapies": significant ↑ dizziness in P-A pathway, ↑ nausea in D-P pathway, ↑ dry mouth in A-P pathway (specific effect sizes for these AE comparisons not given in abstract — `NEEDS_FULLTEXT`).
  - Trial's own conclusion (verbatim, partial): "all three treatment pathways and monotherapies had similar analgesic efficacy. Combination treatment was well tolerated and led to improved pain relief in patients with suboptimal pain control with a monotherapy."
  - HTA companion report adds: most-preferred pathway P-A (pregabalin supplemented with amitriptyline) **43%**, D-P **33%**, A-P **24%**, **P = 0.26** (not significant); P-A pathway had fewest monotherapy discontinuations due to AEs; QALY/cost differences small and not decisively in favor of any pathway (no single pathway superior in cost-effectiveness).
  - No placebo arm (justified by trial authors on ethical/feasibility grounds, citing pre-existing placebo-controlled evidence for each drug individually).
- **Evidence classification:** `DIRECT EVIDENCE`, DPN-specific, head-to-head 3-pathway RCT — currently the largest/longest head-to-head crossover trial in this population per its own abstract's characterization.
- **Critical caution for synthesis:** OPTION-DM found **no significant difference** between any pathway containing pregabalin as either the base or add-on drug and pathways led by amitriptyline or duloxetine — i.e., **no comparative superiority for pregabalin-containing pathways over the alternatives.** This is a key counter-balance source against any sponsor-directional claim that pregabalin-based regimens outperform amitriptyline- or duloxetine-based regimens; it must be surfaced prominently, not omitted, per the sponsor-bias guardrail and Charter question 4 (which extrapolations are unsupported).

## Cross-source synthesis notes (trials-comparative scope only — no sponsor-facing conclusions drafted)

1. **Drug-identity discipline:** Only T1 (Freeman 2008), T2 (Frontiers 2026 combination SR), T5 (COMBO-DN), and T6 (OPTION-DM) report pregabalin-specific numbers. T3 (Soliman/NeuPSIG 2025) reports only the pooled "α2δ-ligands" class (pregabalin + gabapentin) — this must never be relabeled "pregabalin" in synthesis.
2. **Population-identity discipline:** T1, T2, T5, T6 are DPN-specific populations. T3 is general/mixed-etiology neuropathic pain (DPN is presumably a subset of its 313 trials but no DPN-only subgroup estimate was found in the abstract) — any DPN-specific claim sourced from T3 is `INDIRECT EVIDENCE`/extrapolation and must be labeled as such.
3. **Head-to-head comparative evidence (T5, T6) does not show pregabalin superiority** over duloxetine or amitriptyline-based regimens; T5's exploratory finding actually favors duloxetine over pregabalin at the doses tested (uncorrected P<0.001). This directly bears on Research Charter question 4 ("which... only... unproven extrapolation") and must be included in any moderator brief to avoid selective-reporting/sponsor bias.
4. **Combination evidence is consistently framed as modest/uncertain, not as proven synergy:** T2 (GRADE low-certainty, small N), T5 (primary outcome not significant, P=0.370), T6 (combination phase improved pain more than staying on monotherapy, but this is a within-arm before/after comparison, not a placebo-controlled combination-vs-monotherapy RCT result at the primary endpoint level — `NEEDS_FULLTEXT` if a formal combination-vs-monotherapy contrast statistic exists beyond the phase-level descriptive numbers already extracted).
5. **Open gaps carried forward (not blocking this report, per source-provenance's note in the register):**
   - `NEEDS_FULLTEXT`: funding/COI statements and individual-trial risk-of-bias detail for T1, T2 (esp. the QI 2022 included trial), and T3's DPN-relevant subset.
   - `NEEDS_FULLTEXT`: OPTION-DM site-count discrepancy (13 vs 21) between the Lancet paper and the HTA monograph — flagged, not resolved.
   - `NEEDS_SOURCE`: any more recent (post-2025) network meta-analysis directly comparing pregabalin, gabapentin, duloxetine, amitriptyline, and sodium-channel blockers head-to-head across DPN-specific trials was not identified this wave; T3 is the closest available synthesis but is class-pooled and mixed-etiology as noted above.
