## Indications (Section 1)

> "LYRICA is indicated for: Management of neuropathic pain associated with diabetic peripheral neuropathy; Management of postherpetic neuralgia; Adjunctive therapy for the treatment of partial-onset seizures in patients 1 month of age and older; Management of fibromyalgia; Management of neuropathic pain associated with spinal cord injury" — Section 1, p.3.

## Dosage and administration — diabetic peripheral neuropathy (Section 2.2)

> "The maximum recommended dose of LYRICA is 100 mg three times a day (300 mg/day) in patients with creatinine clearance of at least 60 mL/min. Begin dosing at 50 mg three times a day (150 mg/day). The dose may be increased to 300 mg/day within 1 week based on efficacy and tolerability. Although LYRICA was also studied at 600 mg/day, there is no evidence that this dose confers additional significant benefit and this dose was less well tolerated. In view of the dose-dependent adverse reactions, treatment with doses above 300 mg/day is not recommended." — Section 2.2, p.3.

Taper requirement, general (Section 2.1): "When discontinuing LYRICA, taper gradually over a minimum of 1 week [see Warnings and Precautions (5.4)]." — p.3.

Renal-adjustment cross-reference (Section 2.1): "Because LYRICA is eliminated primarily by renal excretion, adjust the dose in adult patients with reduced renal function [see Dosage and Administration (2.7)]." — p.3.

## Warnings and Precautions (Section 5) — verbatim extracts with locators

- **5.1 Angioedema**, p.6: "There have been postmarketing reports of angioedema in patients during initial and chronic treatment with LYRICA... reports of life-threatening angioedema with respiratory compromise requiring emergency treatment. Discontinue LYRICA immediately in patients with these symptoms."
- **5.2 Hypersensitivity**, p.6.
- **5.3 Suicidal Behavior and Ideation**, pp.6–7: "Antiepileptic drugs (AEDs), including pregabalin, the active ingredient in LYRICA, increase the risk of suicidal thoughts or behavior in patients taking these drugs for any indication." Pooled analysis of 199 placebo-controlled trials, 11 AEDs: adjusted Relative Risk 1.8 (95% CI 1.2, 2.7); incidence 0.43% (27,863 AED-treated) vs 0.24% (16,029 placebo-treated); ≈1 additional case per 530 patients treated; 4 suicides in drug-treated patients, 0 in placebo-treated patients in the pooled trials (numbers too small for conclusions on drug effect on suicide). **Table 3** (Risk by Indication): Epilepsy RR 3.5 (risk difference 2.4/1000); Psychiatric RR 1.5 (2.9/1000); Other RR 1.9 (0.9/1000); Total RR 1.8 (1.9/1000).
- **5.4 Increased Risk of Adverse Reactions with Abrupt or Rapid Discontinuation**, p.8: "withdraw LYRICA gradually to minimize the potential of increased seizure frequency... Following abrupt or rapid discontinuation of LYRICA, some patients reported symptoms including insomnia, nausea, headache, anxiety, hyperhidrosis, and diarrhea... Suicidal behavior and ideation have also been reported in patients after discontinuation of pregabalin... If LYRICA is discontinued, taper the drug gradually over a minimum of 1 week rather than discontinue the drug abruptly."
- **5.5 Respiratory Depression**, p.8 — full paragraph: "There is evidence from case reports, human studies, and animal studies associating LYRICA with serious, life-threatening, or fatal respiratory depression when co-administered with central nervous system (CNS) depressants, including opioids, or in the setting of underlying respiratory impairment. When the decision is made to co-prescribe LYRICA with another CNS depressant, particularly an opioid, or to prescribe LYRICA to patients with underlying respiratory impairment, monitor patients for symptoms of respiratory depression and sedation, and consider initiating LYRICA at a low dose. The management of respiratory depression may include close observation, supportive measures, and reduction or withdrawal of CNS depressants (including LYRICA). There is more limited evidence from case reports, animal studies, and human studies associating LYRICA with serious respiratory depression, without co-administered CNS depressants or without underlying respiratory impairment." **Not a boxed warning** — a numbered Warnings and Precautions subsection (this is current as of the 04/2025 revision, consistent with FDA's December 2019 gabapentinoid respiratory-depression communication; the label itself, not the 2019 announcement, is cited as the primary source).
- **5.6 Dizziness and Somnolence**, p.8: dizziness 30% LYRICA vs 8% placebo; somnolence 23% LYRICA vs 8% placebo (adult controlled trials, all indications pooled); generally began shortly after initiation, more frequent at higher doses; led to withdrawal in 4% each; among those reporting these reactions, dizziness persisted until last dose in 30%, somnolence in 42%.
- **5.7 Peripheral Edema**, p.9: incidence 6% LYRICA vs 2% placebo (adult controlled trials); 0.5% LYRICA vs 0.2% placebo withdrew for peripheral edema. Thiazolidinedione co-administration: peripheral edema 3% (2/60) thiazolidinedione-only, 8% (69/859) LYRICA-only, 19% (23/120) both; weight gain 0% (0/60), 4% (35/859), 7.5% (9/120) respectively. "As the thiazolidinedione class of antidiabetic drugs can cause weight gain and/or fluid retention, possibly exacerbating or leading to heart failure, exercise caution when co-administering LYRICA and these agents." **Heart failure caution:** "Because there are limited data on congestive heart failure patients with New York Heart Association (NYHA) Class III or IV cardiac status, exercise caution when using LYRICA in these patients." (This caution is general, not conditioned on thiazolidinedione co-use.)
- **5.8 Weight Gain**, pp.9–10: ≥7% weight gain over baseline in 9% LYRICA vs 2% placebo (≤14-week trials); 0.3% withdrew for weight gain; not associated with baseline BMI, gender, or age; long-term cardiovascular effects of the weight gain are unknown. In diabetic patients specifically: mean weight gain 1.6 kg (range −16 to 16 kg) on LYRICA vs 0.3 kg (range −10 to 9 kg) on placebo; in a cohort of 333 diabetic patients treated ≥2 years, average weight gain was 5.2 kg. "LYRICA treatment did not appear to be associated with loss of glycemic control (as measured by HbA1C)" in controlled/longer-term open-label trials — stated as not systematically assessed for cardiovascular effects.
- **5.9 Tumorigenic Potential**, p.10: unexpectedly high hemangiosarcoma incidence in two mouse strains in lifetime carcinogenicity studies; clinical significance unknown; 57 patients with new/worsening tumors reported among 6396 patient-years >12 years of age, with no background-incidence comparator available.
- **5.10 Ophthalmological Effects**, pp.10–11: blurred vision 7% LYRICA vs 2% placebo; in prospective ophthalmologic testing (>3600 patients): visual acuity reduced 7% vs 5%, visual field changes 13% vs 12%, funduscopic changes 2% vs 2%.
- **5.11 Creatine Kinase Elevations**, p.11: mean CK change 60 U/L (LYRICA) vs 28 U/L (placebo); 1.5% vs 0.7% with CK ≥3× ULN; 3 rhabdomyolysis cases reported premarketing (causality not established — confounders present).
- **5.12 Decreased Platelet Count**, p.11: mean maximal decrease 20×10³/µL (LYRICA) vs 11×10³/µL (placebo); 3% vs 2% with clinically significant decrease (≥20% below baseline and <150×10³/µL); 1 case of severe thrombocytopenia (<20×10³/µL); no increase in bleeding-related adverse reactions in RCTs.
- **5.13 PR Interval Prolongation**, p.11: mean PR increase 3–6 msec at doses ≥300 mg/day; not associated with increased risk of ≥25% PR increase, PR >200 msec, or 2nd/3rd-degree AV block in the analyses performed (limited subgroup sizes noted as a caveat).

## Adverse reactions specific to DPN trials (Section 6.1, Table 4, pp.13–14)

Controlled trials in adults with neuropathic pain associated with diabetic peripheral neuropathy (N=1438 total across arms: 75/150/300/600 mg/day + All PGB [N=979] + Placebo [N=459]):
- Discontinuation for adverse reactions: 9% LYRICA vs 4% placebo; most common reasons: dizziness (3%), somnolence (2%).
- Selected Table 4 rates (All PGB% vs Placebo%): dizziness 21 vs 5; somnolence 12 vs 3; peripheral edema 9 vs 2; weight gain 4 vs 0; edema 2 vs 0; ataxia 3 vs 1; blurry vision 4 vs 2; dry mouth 5 vs 1; confusion 2 vs 1; hypoglycemia 2 vs 1 (full dose-stratified table preserved in source PDF; do not re-derive figures not listed here without re-opening the source).
- 12.4% pregabalin-treated vs 9.0% placebo-treated patients (bridging PHN-trial summary statement on the same page range) had ≥1 severe adverse reaction; 8% vs 4.3% had ≥1 severe treatment-related adverse reaction (this specific 12.4/9.0/8/4.3 statement appears in the PHN-trials narrative, not the DPN-specific narrative — flagged here so it is not misattributed to the DPN population by a later drafting pass).

## Drug interactions (Section 7, p.23)

> "Since LYRICA is predominantly excreted unchanged in the urine, undergoes negligible metabolism in humans (less than 2% of a dose recovered in urine as metabolites), and does not bind to plasma proteins, its pharmacokinetics are unlikely to be affected by other agents through metabolic interactions or protein binding displacement... no pharmacokinetic interactions between pregabalin and... carbamazepine, valproic acid, lamotrigine, phenytoin, phenobarbital, and topiramate."

Pharmacodynamic interaction: "Multiple oral doses of LYRICA were co-administered with oxycodone, lorazepam, or ethanol. Although no pharmacokinetic interactions were seen, additive effects on cognitive and gross motor functioning were seen when LYRICA was co-administered with these drugs. No clinically important effects on respiration were seen [in this specific interaction study]." — note this respiration sub-finding is narrower than, and does not contradict, the broader Section 5.5 respiratory-depression warning, which is based on case reports/postmarketing data, not this PK/PD interaction study; both are preserved here to avoid the drafting error of citing only the reassuring sentence.

## Use in Specific Populations (Section 8)

- **8.1 Pregnancy — Risk Summary**, pp.23–25: Pregnancy Exposure Registry: North American Antiepileptic Drug (NAAED) Pregnancy Registry, 1-888-233-2334, http://www.aedpregnancyregistry.org/. "Observational studies on the use of Lyrica during pregnancy suggest a possible small increase in the rate of overall major birth defects, but there was no consistent or specific pattern of major birth defects identified." Database study: 2,700 first-trimester pregabalin-monotherapy pregnancies vs 3,063,251 unexposed; prevalence ratio for major malformations overall 1.14 (95% CI 0.96–1.35) for pregabalin, vs 1.29 (95% CI 1.01–1.65) lamotrigine, 1.39 (95% CI 1.07–1.82) duloxetine, 1.24 (95% CI 1.00–1.54) lamotrigine-or-duloxetine combined. Two-database published study: Database 1 — 353 pregabalin-exposed vs 368,489 unexposed, adjusted RR 0.87 (95% CI 0.53–1.42), no increased risk; Database 2 — 118 pregabalin-exposed vs 380,347 unexposed, adjusted RR 1.26 (95% CI 0.64–2.49), CI crosses null. Background risk in U.S. general population: major birth defects 2–4%, miscarriage 15–20% of clinically recognized pregnancies. Animal data: fetal structural abnormalities/developmental toxicity in rats/rabbits at AUC ≥16× human exposure at MRD 600 mg/day; neonatal withdrawal syndrome reported in gabapentinoid-exposed newborns when opioids were also used close to delivery (no comparative epidemiologic studies isolating pregabalin alone).
- **8.2 Lactation — Risk Summary**, p.26: pregabalin detected in breast milk at ~76% of maternal plasma steady-state concentration (10 lactating women, ≥12 weeks postpartum, LYRICA 150 mg q12h × 4 doses); estimated infant dose 0.31 mg/kg/day, ≈7% of maternal weight-adjusted dose; effect on milk production not evaluated; infants did not receive the collected milk, so effect on breastfed infant not evaluated. "Because of the potential risk of tumorigenicity, breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment with LYRICA."
- **8.3 Females and Males of Reproductive Potential — Infertility (males)**, pp.26–27: RCT (pregabalin ≤600 mg/day, n=111, vs placebo n=109, 13 weeks + 13-week washout): ~9% (6/65) pregabalin PP-population subjects vs 3% (2/62) placebo had ≥50% reduction in sperm concentration from baseline at Week 26 (primary endpoint); difference within pre-specified 20% non-inferiority margin; no adverse effect on sperm morphology, motility, FSH, or testosterone; reduction reversible by 3 months off-drug in nearly all affected subjects (one subject's semen analysis still showed ≥50% reduction at 9 and 12 months off-drug — clinical relevance unknown).
- **8.4 Pediatric Use**, pp.27–28: safety/effectiveness in DPN, PHN, and spinal-cord-injury neuropathic pain **not established** in pediatric patients. Fibromyalgia: not established as safety/effectiveness endpoint (numerically greater but not statistically significant improvement in a 107-patient, 15-week, ages 12–17 trial). Seizure adjunctive therapy established for ages 1 month–17 years via separate trials (efficacy/dose-response data retained in file but out of DPN-safety scope; see `20_EVIDENCE/trials-comparative/` if seizure data are needed elsewhere).
- **8.5 Geriatric Use**, p.29: DPN trials — 246 patients aged 65–74, 73 patients ≥75; PHN trials — 282 aged 65–74, 379 ≥75; "No overall differences in safety and efficacy were observed between these patients and younger patients" (stated for the pooled indications, not DPN-isolated). Fibromyalgia trials (106 patients ≥65): more frequent neurological adverse reactions in ≥65 group — dizziness, blurred vision, balance disorder, tremor, confusional state, coordination abnormal, lethargy (fibromyalgia-specific finding, flagged as not necessarily generalizable to the DPN population without further verification). General statement: "the risk of toxic reactions to LYRICA may be greater in patients with impaired renal function" and renal function commonly declines with age — cross-referenced to Section 2.7.
- **8.6 Renal Impairment**, p.29: dose adjustment recommended per Section 2.7; not studied in pediatric patients with renal impairment.

## Drug Abuse and Dependence (Section 9, pp.29–30)

- **9.1 Controlled Substance**: "LYRICA is a **Schedule V controlled substance**." Not known to be active at receptor sites associated with drugs of abuse; recommend evaluating patients for history of drug abuse and observing for signs of misuse/abuse (tolerance, dose escalation, drug-seeking behavior).
- **9.2 Abuse**: recreational-user study (N=15, sedative/hypnotic and alcohol users): LYRICA 450 mg single dose rated similarly to diazepam 30 mg single dose on "good drug effect," "high," and "liking." In controlled trials (>5500 patients), 4% LYRICA-treated vs 1% placebo-treated reported euphoria as an adverse reaction; reporting rate ranged 1–12% in some studied populations.
- **9.3 Dependence**: abrupt/rapid discontinuation associated with insomnia, nausea, headache, diarrhea (clinical trials — "consistent with physical dependence"); postmarketing additionally reports seizures, depression, suicidal ideation/behavior, agitation, confusion, disorientation, psychotic symptoms, anxiety, sweating, tremor, pain, dizziness, malaise on withdrawal.

## Postmarketing experience (Section 6.2, pp.22–23)

> "There are postmarketing reports of life-threatening or fatal respiratory depression in patients taking LYRICA with opioids or other CNS depressants, or in the setting of underlying respiratory impairment."

> "There are postmarketing reports of events related to reduced lower gastrointestinal tract function (e.g., intestinal obstruction, paralytic ileus, constipation) when LYRICA was co-administered with medications that have the potential to produce constipation, such as opioid analgesics."

> "There are postmarketing reports of withdrawal symptoms after discontinuation of pregabalin. Reported adverse reactions include, but are not limited to, seizures, depression, suicidal ideation and behavior, agitation, confusion, disorientation, psychotic symptoms, anxiety, insomnia, nausea, pain, sweating, tremor, headache, dizziness, malaise, and diarrhea."

## Overdosage (Section 10, p.30)

Most common postmarketing overdose findings: reduced consciousness, depression/anxiety, confusional state, agitation, restlessness; seizures and heart block also reported; deaths reported in lone-LYRICA overdose and in combination with other CNS depressants. No specific antidote. LYRICA removable by hemodialysis (~50% clearance in 4 hours).
